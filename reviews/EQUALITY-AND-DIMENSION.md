## 1. Feasibility, exact extra conditions, and statement

This extension is feasible, but not merely because the m4 and m5 kernels have
the same displayed entries. It uses the following exact additional facts:

1. For every finite n>=5, the full G0 and G1 kernels are **exactly** the spans
   specified below, rather than only containing those spans.
2. H0,H1 and the scalar G2,H2 are strictly positive; G3=H3=0. Thus the only
   slack-Gram kernel is the skew quadratic zero-row-sum module.
3. The selected m5 branch satisfies0<a<1.

The first two follow, conditional on the independently accepted certificate
bridge, because all Bernstein coefficients are PSD and the last coefficient
is PD: every reduced block is PD throughout the physical interval. The
positive scalar factors and invertible congruences preserve strictness.
The third fact is proved below. Merely knowing the original kernel equations
without strictness would not justify this equality characterization.

For n>=5 set

    Delta=sqrt(20n^2-60n+49),
    a=n(6n-9+Delta)/[16(n-1)(n-2)],
    L=n-(n-2)a,      ell=L/n,
    h=a/n=(1-ell)/(n-2),      b=1/(n-1).

Let A_i>=0, sum A_i<=nI on a finite-dimensional real or complex space, and
write X_i=A_i/n, S=sum X_i<=I. Let C5(n) be the sharp raw constant specified
in the separate theorem-bridge audit. Conditional on the exact certificate
and the stated representation semantics, a unit vector e attains
D5(A)e=-C5(n)e **if and only if**

    Se=e,                        SX_i e=X_i e,                 (1)
    X_i^2 e=ell X_i e,                                         (2)
    (X_i X_j+X_j X_i)e=2h(X_i e+X_j e-b e),   i!=j.           (3)

Every attaining tuple satisfies dim ker(I-S)>=n-1. The minimum ambient
attaining dimension is exactly n-1 over both R and C, with the explicit
real construction in section5. At minimum dimension one necessarily has
S=I on the whole space. This is a vector-condition theorem, not a complete
classification of all attaining tuples or a quantitative stability theorem.

No m4 stationary identity or elementary m4 sum of squares is imported here.
The kernel argument below is parameterized by a; its only branch-specific
inequality needed for the dimension argument is0<a<1.

## 2. Exact kernel-coordinate description

Work temporarily in the raw A normalization and put

    R=sum_i A_i<=nI,   T=nI-R>=0,   r=n/(n-1),
    beta=(n-2)a/(n-1),
    gamma=a[2(n-2)a-n]/(n-1)=a(2beta-r).

The two useful algebraic identities are

    beta(L-a)=a(L-r),                 gamma=a(2beta-r).        (4)

They hold for every a, independently of the m5 stationary equation.

Fix a marked label k. In the source representative order, the trivial kernel
is generated by

    qT=(1,L,beta,L^2,beta L,beta L,beta L,gamma),

and the two standard kernel directions are

    qS=(1,a,a,L,a,0),
    qK=(0,n-1,-(n-1),0,0,1).

The symmetric scalar sector is positive; the skew scalar sector is zero.
By the complete invariant-word decomposition, a vector-valued degree<=2
word array y in ker G_k tensor the ambient space has the following form.
There are ambient vectors f, xi_j,zeta_j for j!=k, with sum xi_j=sum zeta_j=0,
and a skew array Z_ij on active labels with sum_(j!=k) Z_ij=0, such that

    y_empty=f,                 y_k=L f,
    y_j=beta f+xi_j,            j!=k,
    y_kk=L^2 f,
    y_kj=beta L f+a xi_j+(n-1)zeta_j,
    y_jk=beta L f+a xi_j-(n-1)zeta_j,
    y_jj=beta L f+L xi_j,
    y_ij=gamma f+a(xi_i+xi_j)+(zeta_i-zeta_j)+Z_ij,
                                    i!=j, i,j!=k.             (5)

Here Z_ij=-Z_ji and Z_ii=0. These are coefficients in the original word
basis. They follow from the indicator trivial representatives, the standard
representatives with weights c_j, c_i+c_j and c_i-c_j, and the skew
zero-row-sum pair module. The blocks are nonorthonormal contractions, but
PSD and exact contracted kernels imply the corresponding full kernel
vectors Rq; no inverse representative norm is inserted into(5).

More explicitly, if V is the matrix whose columns are the original-word
representatives for one contracted block, its block is M=V^*G_k V.
For q in ker M, positivity of the full G_k gives
(Vq)^*G_k(Vq)=0 and hence G_k Vq=0. Applying this to each zero-sum
standard weight vector gives the full standard kernel copies. Conversely,
representation completeness and the strict reduced block ranks show that
these copies, the one trivial direction, and the skew scalar module exhaust
the full kernel. Thus(5) describes full Gram coordinates, not coordinates
obtained by falsely identifying a contracted matrix with G_k. The symbol Rq
in the preceding sentence denotes this representative embedding Vq, not
the operator sum R used elsewhere in this note.

Likewise, ker H consists exactly of arrays supported on quadratic distinct
words, skew in the two letters and with zero row sums. In particular every
constant, linear, diagonal-quadratic and symmetric-quadratic coordinate of
an array in ker H is zero.

## 3. Equality implies the vector identities

At equality every term in the positive localizer identity has zero quadratic
form. Therefore

    y_w=A_k^(1/2) w(A)e   belongs to ker G_k tensor the space,
    z_w=T^(1/2) w(A)e     belongs to ker H tensor the space.   (6)

The constant and linear z coordinates vanish, so Te=TA_i e=0. This gives
Re=ne and RA_i e=nA_i e, exactly the normalized saturation conditions(1).

The marked linear coordinate in(5) says
A_k^(1/2)A_k e=L A_k^(1/2)e. Multiplying by A_k^(1/2) gives
A_k^2e=L A_k e, hence(2).

For distinct i,j define the raw symmetric defect

    E_ij=A_i A_j+A_j A_i-2a(A_i+A_j-rI).                     (7)

For mark k=i, the two marked/active coordinates in(5) give

    y_ij+y_ji=2beta L f+2a xi_j
               =2a(y_i+y_j-rf),

where the last equality uses beta(L-a)=a(L-r). The same holds for mark k=j.
For a mark k different from i and j, the two active coordinates give

    y_ij+y_ji=2gamma f+2a(xi_i+xi_j)
               =2a(y_i+y_j-rf)

by gamma=a(2beta-r). Thus A_k^(1/2)E_ij e=0 for every k.
The slack vector has zero symmetric quadratic, linear and constant
coordinates, so T^(1/2)E_ij e=0 as well. Consequently

    n ||E_ij e||^2
      =sum_k ||A_k^(1/2)E_ij e||^2+||T^(1/2)E_ij e||^2=0.

Hence E_ij e=0, which after division by n^2 is(3). This use of the slack
kernel is essential on the full domain: knowing only R E_ij e=0 would not
permit deleting the defect without also controlling T.

## 4. Converse and the complex Gram-rank obstruction

Assume(1)--(3), expressed in raw variables. Fix k and define

    f=A_k^(1/2)e,
    xi_j=A_k^(1/2)A_j e-beta f,
    zeta_j=A_k^(1/2)(A_k A_j-A_j A_k)e/[2(n-1)],  j!=k.       (8)

The quadratic condition gives A_k(A_k-LI)e=0. Since ker A_k equals
ker A_k^(1/2), it follows that y_k=Lf and y_kk=L^2f. Saturation and
R e=ne give sum xi_j=(n-L-(n-1)beta)f=0. Saturation also gives
[A_k,R]e=0 and hence sum zeta_j=0. For any active j, y_jj=L y_j.
The symmetric parts of y_kj,y_jk and y_ij,y_ji follow from(7) and(4),
giving the corresponding symmetric parts in(5).

Put d_ij=(y_ij-y_ji)/2 for active i,j. Summing over the active second
index and using [A_i,R]e=0 gives

    sum_(j!=k) d_ij
       = A_k^(1/2)[A_i,R-A_k]e/2
       = A_k^(1/2)[A_k,A_i]e/2=(n-1)zeta_i.

Since sum zeta_j=0, the skew array
Z_ij=d_ij-(zeta_i-zeta_j) has zero row sums. Thus all y coordinates have
exactly the form(5), proving that every G-localizer term annihilates e.

For the slack vector, saturation makes z_empty and z_i zero. The quadratic
condition makes z_ii zero, and(7) makes z_ij+z_ji zero. Finally

    sum_j z_ij=T^(1/2) A_i R e=nT^(1/2) A_i e=0.

Thus z lies in the skew zero-row-sum kernel of H. Every localizer term has
zero quadratic form, and the universal positive identity implies vector
equality at the endpoint. This proves the converse without using a degree4
identity or assuming that degree5 products commute.

We next verify0<a<1 for the selected larger root. It is a positive root of

    F_n(x)=16(n-1)(n-2)x^2-6n(2n-3)x+n^2.

The vertex is less than1 because
16(n-1)(n-2)-3n(2n-3)=10n^2-39n+32>0 for n>=5.
Also F_n(1)=5n^2-30n+32>=7>0, and the two roots are real positive.
Because1 is to the right of the vertex and has positive polynomial value,
the larger root is strictly below1.

Now let v_i=X_i e and p_i=<e,X_i e>>=0. Conditions(1)--(2) give

    sum v_i=e,       sum p_i=1,       ||v_i||^2=ell p_i.

Testing(3) against e gives Re<v_i,v_j>=h(p_i+p_j-b). In the complex case,
testing the same relation against v_i and using X_i v_i=ell v_i yields

    (ell-2h)<v_i,v_j>
       =2h(ell-b)p_i-<v_i,X_j v_i>,

which is real. Here ell-2h=1-a>0, so every Gram entry is real. With
delta=1-a, the Gram matrix M=(<v_i,v_j>) is

    M=diag(delta p_i+hb)
        +h(p 1^T+1 p^T-b 1 1^T).

For nonzero z with sum z_i=0,

    z^*Mz=sum_i(delta p_i+hb)|z_i|^2>0.

Thus rank M>=n-1. Each v_i lies in ker(I-S) by saturation, proving the
ambient dimension and saturation-space lower bounds over both fields.

## 5. Explicit attaining tuple in dimension n-1

Put m=n-1 and work in R^m. Choose a unit e and m unit regular-simplex
vectors z_1,...,z_m in e-perp, so sum z_j=0,
z_i^Tz_j=-1/(m-1) for i!=j, and sum z_jz_j^T=mP/(m-1), P=I-ee^T.
Set

    alpha=1/m=b,   d=ell-alpha,   q=(1-ell)/m,
    X_j=q(P-z_jz_j^T)
         +(sqrt(alpha)e+sqrt(d)z_j)(sqrt(alpha)e+sqrt(d)z_j)^T,
                  1<=j<=m,
    X_n=2hP.                                               (9)

Since0<a<1, one has ell>2/n>1/(n-1), so d>0; q,h are positive. Every
matrix in(9) is PSD. The mixed terms cancel on summation. On e-perp the
first m matrices have scalar sum

    [m(m-2)q+md]/(m-1)=1-2h,

and their ee^T coefficient is1. Therefore sum_i X_i=I.

For j<=m, v_j=alpha e+sqrt(alpha d)z_j and the positive rank-one block
has eigenvalue alpha+d=ell. Thus X_j^2e=ell v_j, also true for X_n e=0.
For distinct active i,j,

    X_i z_j=qz_j-sqrt(alpha d)e/(m-1)-(d-q)z_i/(m-1).

The identities alpha-d/(m-1)=h and mq/(m-1)=h give

    (X_iX_j+X_jX_i)e
       =2alpha h e+2h sqrt(alpha d)(z_i+z_j)
       =2h(v_i+v_j-b e).

For the final label,
(X_nX_j+X_jX_n)e=2h sqrt(alpha d)z_j=2h(v_j-b e).
All conditions(1)--(3) hold, so the converse proves attainment. Scaling
A_i=nX_i gives the raw endpoint. This proves exact minimum n-1 over R,C.

### Optional independent word-value check for this construction

The following hand derivation also verifies that(9) attains the known-family
t5(a), without relying on the localizer converse. Work with raw A_i=nX_i,
r=n/(n-1), and m=n-1 active labels. For a fresh active label the two-coordinate
state transfer has trace2a, determinant ra, and initial e coefficient r:

    T0 = [[r, -g/(n-2)], [g, 2a-r]],
    g^2=r(n-2)(r-a),
    alpha_0=1, alpha_1=r,
    alpha_5=8ra^4-8r^2a^3+r^3a^2.

The last label A_n=2aP acts by J=diag(0,2a). The sum over its five possible
positions in a length5 distinct word is

    sum_(j=0)^4 (T0^(4-j) J T0^j)_(11)
       =-4ra^2(6a-r)(r-a).

Indeed the end positions vanish; the remaining off-diagonal products use
(T0^2)_(12)=2a(T0)_(12),
(T0^3)_(12)=(4a^2-ra)(T0)_(12), and their21 counterparts.
There are(m)_5 words avoiding the last label and(m)_4 choices for the active
labels at each fixed included position. Division by(n)_5=n(m)_4 gives

    [(n-5)alpha_5-4ra^2(6a-r)(r-a)]/n
       =8(2-r)a^4-4r(3-r)a^3+r^2a^2=t5(a).

At n=5 the avoiding count(m)_5 is zero, as required. Permutation symmetry of
the active simplex keeps e a one-dimensional invariant vector of D5, so
this coefficient is its eigenvalue. This check is analytical only, not a
new symbolic or numerical run.
